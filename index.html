Case Study — Retail Analytics

The revenue chart looked healthy. The margin chart told the truth.

A relational data model and SQL analysis of 25,000+ global retail orders — built to separate categories that generate revenue from categories that actually make money.

Business context

Global Superstore sells across four market regions. Leadership had sales-by-category reporting — but no view of which categories were actually profitable once discounting was accounted for.

I designed the underlying relational schema, cleaned and joined order, customer, product, and shipment data, and built the SQL layer that powered every downstream metric — including two engineered measures the raw data didn't provide: Gross Profit Margin and Category Market Share.

The objective wasn't another sales dashboard. It was to answer three questions leadership actually needed: where to invest regionally, which categories to protect or cut, and which customers were quietly going inactive.

Data preparation
  • 41,000+ missing postal codes resolved
  • Redundant / whitespace-corrupted columns removed
  • 11-table relational model — orders, customers, products, shipments, category hierarchy
  • Engineered: Gross Profit Margin, Category Market Share
Primary finding

Tables generated real revenue — and lost money on every sale.

Revenue-only reporting ranked Tables as a mid-tier performer. Joining profit against average discount told a different story: at a ~28% average discount, Tables carried a negative profit margin, while Copiers and Phones delivered the strongest profit in the entire catalog at half the discount rate.

This is the kind of gap that only shows up when discount, profit, and volume are queried together — not when categories are ranked by sales alone.

Full findings

Three additional patterns emerged from the same relational model — each pointing to a distinct business action.

Regional

APAC leads, but EMEA & Africa remain whitespace

APAC ($162M), LATAM ($149M), EU ($147M) and US ($145M) are near parity — while EMEA and Africa sit far below, signaling underpenetrated growth markets rather than saturated ones.

Seasonality

Q4 concentrates demand disproportionately

Sales grew from $2.0M (Q1) to $4.3M (Q4); profit grew from $239K to $503K over the same window — a case for shifting marketing spend toward Q4 timing, not flat year-round budgets.

Customer risk

180-day inactivity flag surfaces reactivation targets

A SQL view flags customers with no purchases in 180+ days by category — turning a static customer list into a prioritized, marketable segment rather than a report nobody acts on.

Recommendations

Framed for a business audience — what the data supports doing next, not just what it shows.

Margin
Re-price or delist Tables

At a ~28% average discount and negative profit, Tables is destroying value at volume — a candidate for discount caps or SKU rationalization, not further promotion.

Growth
Shift expansion investment toward EMEA / Africa

Low current penetration relative to the other three regions suggests headroom that flat, region-agnostic spend is currently leaving on the table.

Retention
Route the inactivity list to category-specific campaigns

Rather than a blanket win-back email, target the flagged segment with offers matched to the category they last purchased — the data already supports that level of specificity.
